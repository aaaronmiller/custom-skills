# Mechanism visual-language reference

## Form follows workflow

Begin with a hierarchy map and control dimensions before choosing a skin. A convincing device has a construction logic: frame, subpanels, tracks, fasteners, displays, service access, and material transitions.

## Surface families

A culture or era may define a token set:

- frame material;
- panel material;
- control material;
- label process;
- display technology;
- fastener shape;
- edge treatment;
- wear model;
- accent pigment;
- illumination color.

Examples include carved hardwood and bone inlay, lacquered sheet metal and bakelite, milled aluminum and rubber, glazed ceramic and woven cord, or future composites with projected labels. These are systems, not texture swaps.

## Geometry

Use consistent radii, panel depths, screw sizes, track widths, and control scales. High-consequence controls may be larger, guarded, or spatially isolated. Frequently used controls should be reachable and grouped by operation.

## Lighting and depth

CSS can produce convincing depth with restrained gradients, inset borders, layered shadows, highlights, and occlusion. Keep a consistent light direction. Avoid excessive beveling that reduces legibility.

## Labels

Labels should appear manufactured for the device: engraved, printed, stamped, painted, etched, or displayed. Use concise labels on the surface and richer hover or focus help. Never hide critical meaning solely in tooltips.

## Wear and history

Wear belongs at touched edges, rotating controls, hinges, grips, cable ports, and service seams. Random grunge weakens the illusion. Mechanism history can be represented by repair plates, mismatched modules, hand annotations, or replaced components.

## Negative space

Instrument panels require rest areas. Negative space separates modules, reveals hierarchy, and makes unusual controls meaningful. Do not fill every surface with glyphs, screws, and meters.

## Animation

Moving parts must follow plausible pivots and tracks. A drum rotates by a bounded amount, a shutter slides, a needle accelerates and settles, and gears mesh in opposite directions. Processing animations can accelerate briefly, then stop. Constant decorative movement suggests a toy rather than an instrument.

## Skins and semantics

Keep a semantic DOM and state model below the skin. A future reskin can replace wood with ceramic or circuitry without changing component IDs, value ranges, commands, or serialization.
