# Edge-Seeking Iterations

> Push to the edge, then back off. Extra rounds are justified only by measured gain.

Field pattern from multi-folder survey work. Complements the core loop: where the core loop refines the *artifact*, this refines the *process* each round and rations loops across a corpus.

## Rules

1. **Version the process, not just the artifact.** Each round ends with a retro that rewrites the procedure (`process v1 → v2`); the last version governs a final gap-only pass. Process delta is itself scored: keep what raised gain-per-round, cut the rest.
2. **Don't loop everywhere.** Full treatment for the highest-leverage items only (4–5 per corpus); inventory the rest. A loop must be earned by expected gain, never default.
3. **Never restrain useful complexity.** Thresholds gate *rounds*, never *content*: a round may add structure, not just cut. Restraint applies to iteration count, not idea count.
4. **Back off at the knee.** Track score-vs-round; when delta drops below 0.5/10 (or two flat rounds), record last-good and pivot to the next gap. Stopping one round late beats three rounds late; stopping one round early is fixed by the gap pass.
5. **Pivot log, not stall.** Every exit names: last good version, why stopping, next gap. Silent stalls and scope expansions are both failures.
6. **Budgets force the edge.** Per-item token/turn caps (e.g. 1k–10k out-tokens, ≤3 rounds) make the knee visible instead of theoretical. Log (route, model, effort, rounds, tokens, delta, cost) for the standing audit.
