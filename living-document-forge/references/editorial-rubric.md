# Editorial rubric for concept canvases

## Preserve the author’s useful specificity

Specific images often carry more design information than abstract terminology. “A control surface considered like a fighter cockpit, craft bench, or custom synthesizer” is more useful than “intuitive UI.” Preserve concrete comparisons, then extract their operational implications.

## Convert repetition into structure

Rough ideation repeats important ideas from different angles. Do not delete every repetition. Identify whether each instance contributes:

- a new example;
- a stronger requirement;
- a tension;
- an implementation consequence;
- an emotional reason;
- a future extension.

Consolidate only when no new information remains.

## Separate premise from implementation

A premise explains what experience should exist. An implementation candidate explains one way to achieve it. Keep them distinct so later technology changes do not erase the goal.

## Expose tensions

Strong concept documents contain productive contradictions. Examples:

- autonomous culture versus player authorship;
- historical depth versus invented cultures;
- public voting versus difficult art;
- expensive generation versus accessible hardware;
- persistent style versus novelty;
- gamification versus emotional seriousness.

Do not resolve these with slogans. Place them where future experiments can address them.

## Avoid product-document drift

The canvas is not yet a PRD. Do not turn exploratory sections into exhaustive acceptance criteria unless asked. It may include implementation shape, feasibility, quality doctrine, and MVP boundaries, but it should remain a conceptual workshop.

## Tone

Use direct, precise language with enough imagery to keep the idea alive. Avoid generic startup language, inflated claims, and repetitive “AI-powered” phrasing. Distinguish aspiration from evidence.

## Quality questions

Before finalizing a section, ask:

1. Does the section make one durable argument?
2. Does its deck explain why the section exists?
3. Are examples doing conceptual work?
4. Are uncertainties visible?
5. Could another agent locate the right place for a future edit?
6. Does the wording preserve user intent without preserving accidental transcription errors?
7. Does the section duplicate another section’s job?
8. Can a proposal or experiment be derived from unresolved questions?
