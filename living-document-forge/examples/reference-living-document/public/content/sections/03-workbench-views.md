## Views are questions, not themes

A living document needs multiple views because operators approach it with different questions.

- **Dashboard:** What needs attention now?
- **Document:** What does the current argument say?
- **Search:** Where is this concept discussed?
- **History:** What event occurred, when, and through which actor?
- **Changelog:** What changed in a released version, and why does it matter?

Combining these into one endless page weakens all of them. A timeline embedded between every section interrupts reading. A dashboard that repeats every paragraph becomes a second, less trustworthy document.

## The three-pane workbench

On wide screens, the section index remains on the left, the current view occupies the center, and context-specific controls live on the right. The reader can see where they are, what the active section depends on, and what actions are available without placing controls inside every paragraph.

The center canvas remains dominant. The surrounding rails are intentionally quieter and use rules rather than stacks of floating cards.

At narrower widths, the inspector becomes a drawer. On phones, both rails become drawers so the document receives the full viewport. This is a change in access pattern, not a deletion of capability.
